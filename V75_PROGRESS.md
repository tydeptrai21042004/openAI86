# V75 — paper-specific first tops and stopping cuts

Source-only continuation from V74. The two new modules adapt the pinned reference first-top argument to the manuscript's PaperCore/PaperGood predicates and base-1.1 classes. No new mathematical axioms are intentionally introduced. No Lean compilation, regression tests or verification runner executed. All new proofs are candidates, not certified results.

* `PaperFirstTops.lean`: paper-specific component sizes and side synthesis, size comparison, restriction of the actual block-side subtotal, predecessor nesting, separation of whole CURRENT components, first-top projection rows, side subtotals and size absorption. `PaperRepClass` uses the printed floor-log classes, with hereditary representatives constructed by V70. Successful nodes are enumerated by the actual PaperGood/PaperCore test. `paperSuccModes_cover` supplies coverage, and `PaperRepClass.role_label_sep` exports the shifted slope separation needed in Section 9.
* `PaperTopCount.lean`: uses the new first-top rows in the actual lift composition, sums first-top lengths, and applies the first-crossing cuts to all supplied heights/types/thresholds. `paper_first_top_count_main` calls V73 `paper_linear_energy` with the exact density-chain constant and uses the newly constructed paper-specific representative classes on actual retained pairs. No equality between dyadic and base-1.1 classes is asserted.

The source uses the reference normalization and explicit coefficient/analytic/schedule assumptions (including `24 * CF ≤ Dstar^55` in the final first-top theorem). The theorem statements are conditional on these concrete hypotheses, not an unconditional proof of the printed Proposition 8.12. Section 9 still needs to instantiate the new representative classes inside its progression counts; the complete torus/Fourier expansion, error budgets and final parameter integration are not done. Sections 2–6, the exact LSS inverse theorem, the weighted maximal theorem and remaining analytic hypotheses remain open as in V74. The false printed Lemma 3.5(2) still requires its correction.
